## Why this supersedes 20260912-034727-9204518
That record read pfund/pim3/iip3/lo-leak from FFT bins of an 8001-sample window (`tran 10p 100n 20n`). Its notes state ngspice's `fft` zero-pads and that this was "confirmed". That is build-dependent: this host's ngspice zero-pads (4097-bin labels that do not describe the padded transform), while the batch fleet's ngspice-46 does not (4001 bins). The same deck gave pim3_dbm -78.5 (local) vs -94.0 (batch) and iip3 -20.6 vs -12.6 (issue #135), and the old record does not say which FFT built its numbers. Its pim3_dbm/iip3_dbm values are therefore leakage/scalloping-dominated and not comparable across backends. The window is now exactly 65536 = 2^16 samples over exactly 80 ns (tstep 1.220703125 ps), so padding is a no-op. The old record stays as history; its numbers are not deleted, only superseded in method.

## Result
Partial. pfund_dbm and lo_rf_leak_dbm (and conversion gain) now agree across backends well inside 0.1 dB. pim3_dbm and iip3_dbm do NOT: with the leakage removed, the IM3 read sits on the transient sampler's interpolation-error floor (pim3 keeps falling as the record is refined; tightening reltol to 1e-6 moved it from -137 to -130 dBm locally), so these two numbers are not circuit IM3/IIP3 and must not be used. The 0.1 dB criterion for them is unmet and is left to a follow-up (a method that resolves IM3, e.g. a swept 1:3-slope bench or a stronger drive).
